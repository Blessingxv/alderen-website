AI that carries engineering work from requirement to decision.

Alderen is building Ansel, an AI engineering system that coordinates simulation-driven design. Ansel combines intelligent agents with machine-learning models, including fast surrogate predictions, to streamline the journey from engineering requirements to verified results. Full simulation remains the final check, with engineers retaining control of the final decision.

Simulation shouldn't be the bottleneck in design.

Simulation is how engineers check a design before it is built, but the loop around it is slow. We are talking to engineers to learn exactly where.

Setup takes longer than the thinking
Turning a requirement into a simulation-ready model is hours of manual preparation.
Every change means another full run
Even small design changes can need a complete analysis to see what happened.
Answers arrive after the decision
When results come late, problems are found later and cost more to fix.

One workflow, from request to verified result.

Ansel combines agents that coordinate the workflow with machine-learning models such as surrogates. Surrogate prediction is one capability within it, and full simulation remains the final check.

  1. 1

    Ansel's agents help prepare the work

    Describe the design and its requirements, and Ansel's agents help prepare the setup and coordinate the tools involved. Ansel's agents are being developed to automate parts of this preparation while keeping engineers in control of the assumptions.

  2. 2

    Surrogate models predict fast

    Ansel's surrogate models will give rapid stress and deflection estimates for defined classes of parts. We are building the validation and confidence checks that show when an estimate can be trusted and when a full simulation is needed.

  3. 3

    Full analysis where it counts

    Full simulation and engineering verification stay central to the workflow. When confidence is low or the stakes are high, the work goes to a full analysis, and final sign-off stays with the engineer.

Ansel is in development. It is not yet a deployed product.
