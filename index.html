From simulation setup to results engineers can check.

Alderen is building Ansel, an agentic engineering SaaS platform for FEA and CFD. Ansel is designed to connect simulation preparation, solver runs, machine-learning predictions and result checks in one workflow. The aim is to reduce repetitive work while keeping engineers in control of critical decisions.

The solver is only part of the work.

A simulation can take minutes or hours to run, but getting it ready and checking the result often takes much more effort. Engineers still have to prepare models, deal with failed runs and repeat studies as designs change.

Getting a model ready takes time
CAD cleanup, meshing, material properties and boundary conditions all need attention before the first solver run.
Every design change adds another run
A change in geometry, loading or operating conditions can mean preparing another case, running the solver again and comparing outputs.
A result is not the same as an answer
Convergence, assumptions, model validity and unexpected behaviour still need to be checked before anyone can rely on the result.

Ansel connects the work around the simulation.

Ansel is designed to use agents to coordinate defined engineering tasks, conventional solvers to run the physics and ML surrogate models to assess repeat cases faster once validated for a defined range. Each tool has a specific role, with clear limits and engineer review.

  1. 1

    Prepare the simulation

    Start with the geometry, loads, material or fluid properties and the intended checks. Ansel is designed to organise the setup steps, assist with inputs and flag assumptions that need an engineer to confirm.

  2. 2

    Run the solver or a validated model

    For structural and internal-flow cases, the workflow is designed to connect to FEA and CFD solvers. Once trained and tested for a defined range, PyTorch surrogate models can screen repeat design variants. Cases outside that range go back to full analysis.

  3. 3

    Check the evidence

    Keep a record of the inputs, assumptions, solver outputs and model limits. Ansel is designed to help flag convergence problems or results that need another run. The engineer reviews and approves the final result.

Ansel is in development. The first release focuses on bounded structural FEA and internal-flow CFD cases.

Built by engineers who understand the work.

Alderen is an early-stage engineering software company founded by two mechanical engineers from the University of Johannesburg. Our backgrounds span mechanical design, finite element analysis, computational fluid dynamics, programming and machine learning.

We have worked with engineering analysis tools and understand how much effort goes into preparing a model, checking assumptions and interpreting results. We started Alderen to develop software that handles more of the repetitive work while leaving engineers in control of the analysis.

Our current focus is Ansel, an agentic platform for structural FEA and internal-flow CFD workflows. We are building it alongside customer discovery with engineers working on real simulation problems.
